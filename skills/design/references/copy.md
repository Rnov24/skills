# Copy — voice fixtures and microcopy mechanics

Copy fails in two ways. The visible failure is wording: generic opening
lines, vague buttons, errors that apologise instead of instructing. The
invisible failure is process: a model asked to "write in a playful tone"
produces the distribution average of playful, which reads as nobody's
voice at all. This file attacks both — mechanics for the visible failure,
fixtures for the invisible one.

## The voice-fixture principle

Tone instructions do not transfer voice; examples do. Before writing
copy for a surface, assemble three to five real opening lines from real
products in the target tone and imitate their *specificity* — the named
places, dates, numbers, verticals, and refusals to generalise — never
their wording. A fixture is a line that could only belong to one product.
If a candidate line could sit on any competitor's page unchanged, it is
not specific enough, whatever its tone.

Per-tone sample shapes, condensed from upstream fixtures:

- **Editorial** names a place, a date, or a craft: "Baked at 5 a.m. in a
  wood-fired oven in Yogyakarta." The specificity is the personality.
- **Brutalist** states the mechanism and refuses decoration: "No
  accounts. No tracking. Files are deleted after 24 hours."
- **Soft** admits the user's situation before offering the tool: "Moving
  house is chaos. The checklist doesn't have to be."
- **Technical** leads with the constraint or the number: "Cold start:
  41ms. p99 latency budget enforced in CI."
- **Luxury** underclaims and lets scarcity do the work: "Twelve pieces.
  No restock."
- **Playful** commits to one concrete joke with a straight face rather
  than sprinkling exclamation marks over plain sentences.
- **Austere** removes until only the fact remains: "Est. 2019.
  Independent. No advertising."

## Banned opening lines

These openings are banned outright; each is the distribution average
wearing a costume.

| Banned | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "Built for the modern team" | Names no team, no era, no product |
| "Unleash your …" | Borrowed energy, zero information |
| "Where X meets Y" | A formula, not a claim |
| "Empower your …" | The user does things; the product does not empower |
| "Reimagine the way you …" | Announces novelty instead of showing it |
| "Supercharge your workflow" | Two clichés in three words |
| "Seamless integration" | Claims the one thing users cannot verify |
| "In today's digital landscape" | Filler that dates the page on arrival |

When the brief gives nothing specific enough to open with, ask one
question rather than inventing specificity. Invented specificity —
fabricated customers, metrics, places — is worse than a plain line, and
it is a honesty failure, not a style choice (see the honest-copy rule in
taste-core.md's Imagery section and the input-geometry of claims in the
review rubric).

## Microcopy mechanics

- Buttons carry specific verbs: "Save changes", "Create account",
  "Download invoice". Never "OK", "Submit", "Click here", or a bare
  "Yes"/"No" on a destructive confirmation — the button restates the
  action it performs.
- Pick one word per action and use it everywhere: Delete or Remove,
  Sign in or Log in, Settings or Preferences. Synonym drift reads as
  two products stitched together.
- An error message has three parts in order: what happened, why, and
  what to do next. No "Oops!", no humour in a frustration path, no
  exclamation marks. "That email is already registered. Try signing in,
  or use a different address." is the shape.
- An empty state names what is empty, says why it matters, and offers
  exactly one button that starts filling it. Never a bare "No items."
- Loading copy scales with the wait: under a second, nothing; a few
  seconds, a neutral present-tense label ("Loading invoices…"); long
  waits, a progress indication and permission to leave.
- Typography is part of copy: curly quotes, real apostrophes, en dashes
  for ranges, em dashes for breaks, an ellipsis character rather than
  three periods, and a non-breaking space before units (5 kg, 100 ms).

## Re-grounding fixtures per product language

The sample shapes above are English because their sources are. Fixtures
do not translate: a line imitated through translation carries the source
language's rhythm into the target and reads as dubbed. For a product in
another language — Indonesian products in particular — build the fixture
set from real products writing natively in that language, in the target
tone, and imitate their specificity the same way. The mechanics in this
file (specific verbs, three-part errors, one word per action) are
language-independent; the fixtures never are.
